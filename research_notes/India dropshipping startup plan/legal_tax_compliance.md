## Q1. Is GST registration mandatory for a store that sells only through its own Shopify website (not Amazon/Flipkart)? Section 24, ECO status, thresholds, inter-state rules

### Takeaway
On paper, an own-website seller is **not** caught by Section 24's e-commerce clause, because Shopify does not collect payment or TCS for the merchant. In practice, a dropshipping store that ships **to customers in other states** must register for GST from the **first inter-state sale**, whatever its turnover, under Section 24(i). The ₹40 lakh / ₹20 lakh thresholds only help if every sale stays inside the seller's own state. The Oct 2023 relief for small sellers covers only sales made **through marketplaces (ECOs)**, and only within one state. It does not help an own-website seller.

### Cited Findings
**Section 24 and own website vs marketplace**
- Section 24(i) requires registration for persons making **any inter-State taxable supply**, notwithstanding the Section 22(1) turnover threshold. Explainers describe it as applying "from the first rupee". — [KnowYourGST – Section 24 text](https://www.knowyourgst.com/gstlaw/the-central-goods-and-services-tax-act-2017/24-compulsory-registration-in-certain-cases-24/); [TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/compulsory-registration-gst-section-24); [GSTGyaan](https://gstgyaan.com/section-24-person-who-is-compulsorily-required-registration-under-gst)
- The relief for inter-state suppliers below the threshold covers **services**, not goods. A separate notification exempts **handicraft goods** suppliers below the threshold (03/2018-Integrated Tax, 22-10-2018). — [GSTGyaan – summary of exemption notifications](https://www.gstgyaan.com/person-exempted-from-registration-summary-of-notifications-issued-under-section-23-2-of-CGST-Act); [TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/compulsory-registration-gst-section-24)
- An entity running its own website is an "Electronic Commerce Operator" under Section 2(45), but it is not liable to register **as an ECO**, because it collects money itself through a payment gateway and Section 52 TCS does not apply. In that case the ordinary thresholds apply, **but selling goods inter-state requires compulsory registration under Section 24(i)**. — [TaxGuru – applicability of GST to online business](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/applicability-gst-online-business-services.html)
- The CBIC-style GST FAQ on e-commerce says that where someone sells its own products through its own website, there is **no requirement to collect TCS**, and the transactions are liable to GST at prevailing rates. The FAQ answers "No" on TCS for a reseller who buys from vendors and sells under its own billing. — [GST FAQ – E-commerce (referencer.in copy)](https://referencer.in/GST/Files/GST-FAQ_E-commerce.pdf)
- Section 52 TCS applies only to supplies made **through a platform by other suppliers, where the operator collects the consideration**. — [Patron Accounting – TCS u/s 52](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/tcs-section-52-ecommerce-gst-explained)
- A compliance guide describes Shopify as SaaS that "does not facilitate the sale by collecting payments on your behalf. Therefore, TCS … does not apply to Shopify store owners". It adds that registration becomes compulsory if the seller **also** lists on Amazon or Flipkart. This is a consultant's view, not an official ruling. Attribution between these two search results is approximate. — [Disytax](https://disytax.com/?p=23810); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/tcs-section-52-ecommerce-gst-explained)
- Selling on Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho and similar marketplaces requires registration from the first sale, because those platforms must collect TCS under Section 52. The one exception is the 2023 intra-state relief below. — [ComplianceCalendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/gst-registration-for-e-commerce-sellers); [Disytax – GST for online sellers](https://disytax.com/gst-for-online-sellers/)

**The Oct 2023 relief for small sellers on ECOs (often misread)**
- **Notification 34/2023-Central Tax (31 Jul 2023, effective 1 Oct 2023)** exempts suppliers of goods **through an ECO** from registration if their aggregate turnover is within the state threshold. The conditions are: **(i) no inter-State supply of goods**; (ii) supply through ECOs in **only one State/UT**; a PAN must be declared on the portal and an **enrolment number** obtained before supplying. — [Notification 34/2023 (GST Council PDF)](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/gst-ct-34-2023.pdf); [CAclub](https://caclub.in/exemption-from-gst-registration-for-supplies-thru-ecom-operators/); [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/notifcation-no-34-2023-central-tax-gst-registration-exemption-to-seller-making-supplies-of-goods-through-an-electronic-commerce-operator.html)
- No 2025 amendment to Section 24(i) turned up. Search results describe the provision as unchanged for FY 2025-26. — [TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/compulsory-registration-gst-section-24); [Cashflo 2025 guide](https://www.cashflo.io/magazine/gst-registration-rules-and-applicability-latest-guide-for-2025)

**Thresholds (intra-state only)**
- Goods: **₹40 lakh** in most states. Goods: **₹20 lakh** in states that opted out under Notification 10/2019-CT, after the 32nd GST Council meeting (Jan 2019). Services: ₹20 lakh. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-registration-limits-increased); [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/feeds/blog/special-category-states-gst-registration)
- Sources **conflict** on which states use the lower limit. All agree Puducherry is at ₹20 lakh. Telangana is listed at ₹40 lakh by one source and ₹20 lakh by another. Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura are in the lower group. The status of Meghalaya, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand is disputed. Assam, HP and J&K use ₹40 lakh. — [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/feeds/blog/special-category-states-gst-registration); [myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/blog/gst-exemption-limit/); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=559124)

**Registration process / cost (2025 change)**
- **New Rule 14A (Notification 18/2025-CT, 31 Oct 2025; effective 1 Nov 2025):** an optional simplified registration. A GSTIN is granted electronically **within 3 working days** of ARN generation, with Aadhaar authentication mandatory for the primary authorised signatory and at least one promoter/partner. It applies where output tax liability does not exceed **₹2.5 lakh/month** (CGST+SGST/UTGST+IGST+cess). Withdrawing from the scheme requires at least 3 months of returns if applied before 1 Apr 2026, and at least 1 tax period if applied on or after 1 Apr 2026. — [Taxo.online – GSTN advisory](https://taxo.online/latest-news/01-11-2025-gstn-has-issued-an-advisory-for-simplified-gst-registration-scheme/); [Taxo.online – Rule 14A insertion](https://taxo.online/latest-news/01-11-2025-amendment-to-gst-rules-insertion-of-new-rule-14a-and-9a-optional-aadhaar-based-registration-and-grant-of-registration-based-on-data-analysis-and-risk-parameters-cbic-notificati/); [Filingin](https://filingin.com/blog/en/gst-new-registration-rule-2025)
- Sources differ on Rule 14A's scope. One summary says eligible applicants make supplies "only to registered persons (B2B)". Others describe the cap as tax payable on supplies **to registered persons**. Under that reading, a B2C store with zero B2B output would trivially qualify. — [Taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-news/01-11-2025-amendment-to-gst-rules-insertion-of-new-rule-14a-and-9a-optional-aadhaar-based-registration-and-grant-of-registration-based-on-data-analysis-and-risk-parameters-cbic-notificati/)

### Inferences
- **The practical answer for this store is yes, register.** A Shopify dropshipping store that accepts orders from anywhere in India makes inter-state taxable supplies of goods, so Section 24(i) applies from day one. Only a store that **geo-restricts** orders to its home state and stays under ₹40 lakh (or ₹20 lakh / ₹10 lakh in some states) could legally stay unregistered. This is rarely practical, because Shopify's default shipping zones cover all of India.
- Section 24's marketplace clause (usually cited as **24(ix)**: persons supplying through an ECO that must collect TCS) does **not** apply to a store on its own Shopify domain. Shopify is a software provider, and the merchant's gateway settles money to the merchant. **Caveat:** if the store **also** lists on Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho, that clause and the marketplaces' TCS do apply.
- Registration on gst.gov.in has **no government fee**. Rule 14A makes approval faster (3 working days). The owner should confirm with a CA whether they file under Rule 14A or under normal Rule 9.
- **Place of supply nuance (confirm with CA):** for a sale with goods movement, the place of supply is where movement ends, at the customer. If the seller is registered in State A and the customer is in State B, IGST applies, even if the supplier physically ships from State C.

### Gaps
- I could not open the CGST Act text or Notification 10/2019-CT to settle which states use ₹20 lakh vs ₹40 lakh for goods. Check Notification 10/2019-CT on cbic-gst.gov.in.
- My recollection of the ₹10 lakh goods threshold in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura was not verified in this session.
- I found no 2024 change specific to small own-website sellers. The only changes found were Notification 34/2023 (ECO sellers, intra-state) and Notification 36/2023 (composition via ECO; see Q2), both effective 1 Oct 2023.
- No typical CA fee for **GST registration** alone was found in reliable sources.

## Q2. Is the GST composition scheme available to an online (own-website) seller?

### Takeaway
The composition scheme is effectively **not usable** for a pan-India dropshipping store. Composition taxpayers **cannot make inter-state outward supplies of goods**. Since 1 Oct 2023 they may sell goods through marketplaces, but only intra-state. Composition dealers also cannot collect GST from customers or claim input tax credit.

### Cited Findings
- Finance Act 2023 removed "goods or" from Section 10(2)(d), letting **goods** suppliers on e-commerce platforms opt into composition. Suppliers of **services** through ECOs remain barred. — [Taxninja](https://taxninja.in/blog/budget-2023-composition-scheme-benefit-to-e-commerce-goods-suppliers); [GSTHero](https://gsthero.com/gst-union-budget-2023-amendments-proposed-in-finance-bill/)
- **Notification 36/2023-CT (4 Aug 2023; effective 1 Oct 2023)** sets a special procedure: the ECO "shall not allow any inter-State supply of goods" by a composition taxpayer, and must collect TCS under Section 52. — [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/cbic-notifies-special-procedure-for-ecos-in-respect-of-supply-of-goods-made-by-composition-dealers); [Fintaxblog](https://fintaxblog.com/cbic-notifies-special-procedure-for-e-commerce-operators-composition-suppliers/)
- Composition basics for traders: turnover ceiling **₹1.5 crore** (₹75 lakh in special-category states); tax **1%** (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST); businesses making **inter-state outward supplies cannot opt in**; dealers issue a **bill of supply**, cannot collect GST and cannot claim ITC; returns are **CMP-08** (quarterly) and **GSTR-4** (annual). — [TAXAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/gst-composition-scheme); [Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/gst/gst-composition-scheme.html); [Masters India](https://www.mastersindia.co/books/indian-gst-a-comprehensive-guide/composition-scheme/)
- Sources conflict on the GSTR-4 due date: 30 June per one source, 30 April per another (older-looking). — [TAXAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/gst-composition-scheme); [myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/blog/composition-scheme-under-gst/)

### Inferences
- The legal bar on inter-state supply is usually cited as Section 10(2)(c) of the CGST Act. I could not confirm the clause number from a source. A store shipping across India therefore needs **regular** GST registration.
- Composition only fits a store that deliberately sells within one state. Even then, the lack of ITC means the GST charged by the supplier becomes a pure cost.

### Gaps
- I could not open the official text of Section 10(2)(c) or Notification 36/2023 (fetching was blocked).

## Q3. GST rates on common dropshipping categories after "GST 2.0" (56th GST Council, Sept 2025)

### Takeaway
Since **22 Sep 2025**, GST runs on two main slabs, **5% and 18%**; the 12% and 28% slabs are gone, plus a 40% demerit slab, which my sources did not detail. For textiles, the 5% band now extends to **₹2,500 per piece** (up from ₹1,000), and apparel above ₹2,500 per piece moved to **18%** (from 12%). For made-up home textiles such as bedsheets, curtains and cushion covers, sources **conflict**: some say a uniform 5%, others say 18% above ₹2,500 per piece. Rates for HSN 9404 (pillows, cushions, mattresses, quilts) could not be confirmed. **Check each product's HSN on the CBIC rate finder before invoicing.**

### Cited Findings
- The 56th GST Council meeting (3 Sep 2025) moved to a two-slab structure of 5% and 18%, abolishing 12% and 28%, effective **22 Sep 2025**. Man-made fibre went from 18% to 5%, and man-made yarn from 12% to 5%. — [Tribune India (MATEXIL)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/chairman-matexil-welcomes-rectification-of-inverted-duty-structure-for-mmf-textiles)
- A Finance Ministry Lok Sabha reply (as reported) says the textile structure was rationalised by removing the 12% slab. Apparel and clothing accessories **above ₹2,500 per piece** rose from 12% to **18%**, and the concessional **5%** threshold was raised from ₹1,000 to **₹2,500** per piece. Some handmade items stay at 5%. — [TaxGuru – GST 2.0 impact on textiles](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/impact-rate-rationalization-gst-2-0-regime-textile-sector.html)
- "Other made-up textile articles, sets" **above ₹2,500 per piece** also moved from 12% to **18%**, effective 22 Sep 2025. — [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gst-rate-rationalisation-on-readymade-garments-to-boost-affordability-and-employment/); [Free Press Journal (PTI)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/garments-above-2500-now-attract-18-gst-industry-warns-of-higher-prices-and-middle-class-impact)
- **Conflict:** one HSN guide says Chapter 63 products carry a "UNIFORM 5% GST rate" from 22 Sep 2025, lists HSN 6302 (bed linen) at 5%, and says the value-based split was dropped. An IncorpX tariff page still maps HSN 6302 10 10 to "5% / 12%". — [Disytax – Chapter 63](https://disytax.com/?p=19268); [IncorpX – HSN 6302](https://www.incorpx.io/hsn-codes/heading-6302)
- A TaxGuru textile guide citing Notification 9/2025 still shows 12% above ₹2,500, which appears outdated. — [TaxGuru – GST guide for textile sector](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-guide-textile-sector-wholesalers-e-commerce-retailers.html)
- **HSN 9404** (mattresses, pillows, cushions, quilts): ClearTax's page shows a rate history of 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% without saying which applies now. Credlix lists 28% for 9404 alongside 5% for some subheadings, with no effective dates. Neither is reliable for the post-Sep-2025 rate. — [ClearTax – HSN 9404](https://cleartax.in/s/mattress-supports-gst-rates-hsn-code-9404); [Credlix – 9404](https://credlix.com/hsn-code/9404)

### Inferences
- For a home-furnishing dropshipping store, the working assumption is: most bedsheets, curtains, cushion covers and towels sold **at or below ₹2,500 per piece** are at **5%**, and pieces **above ₹2,500** are likely at **18%**. This rests on the more specific, newer sources (A2Z Taxcorp and the PTI report). The owner's CA should confirm against Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate) as amended.
- "Per piece" sale value matters for **sets**, such as a bedsheet with pillow covers. Pricing just above ₹2,500 can push an item into 18%, which affects pricing strategy.
- Shipping or COD charges billed to the customer are generally treated as part of a composite supply, taxed at the goods' rate. This is from general knowledge; confirm with CA.

### Gaps
- I could not access the CBIC notification (9/2025-CT(Rate)) or the official 56th Council press release, so the Chapter 63 rate above ₹2,500 and all Chapter 94 rates remain **unconfirmed**. Use the CBIC GST rate finder (cbic-gst.gov.in) by HSN.
- The 40% slab and items outside textiles (e.g. electronics, kitchenware, decor) were not researched.

## Q4. Input tax credit for dropshipping, return filing (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, QRMP), and costs (registration vs CA fees)

### Takeaway
A **registered** dropshipper can claim ITC on the GST charged by its supplier, provided the supplier invoices the store's GSTIN, files its own GSTR-1 so the credit shows in GSTR-2B, and ships "bill-to (store) / ship-to (customer)". An unregistered dropshipper cannot claim ITC, so the supplier's GST becomes a cost. Registration is free. CA fees for monthly GSTR-1 + GSTR-3B for a small proprietorship are commonly quoted at **₹500–₹1,500/month**. Under **QRMP** (turnover up to ₹5 crore), the number of filings drops from 24 to 8 a year.

### Cited Findings
- In a "bill to / ship to" model, the party billed and the party receiving goods differ. ITC flows to the billed party. — [ICMAI – Bill to/Ship to in GST](https://icmai.in/TaxationPortal/upload/IDT/Article_GST/47.pdf)
- A Rajasthan AAR (2018) held ITC of IGST paid under a "bill to ship to" model admissible. — [TaxHeal – AAR summary](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=73187)
- In domestic dropshipping where both merchant and supplier are GST-registered, the merchant pays the supplier inclusive of GST and claims that GST as ITC against output tax on the sale to the customer. — [Swipe – GST for dropshipping](https://getswipe.in/blog/post/gst-for-dropshipping-a-guide-for-indian-businesses)
- For imports from an overseas dropship supplier, IGST is paid under reverse charge and can be claimed as ITC. This is not the main model here. — [TaxAdda](https://taxadda.com/?p=511288)
- CA fee benchmarks are vendor or blog estimates, not authoritative. Quoted figures include:
  - GSTR-1 at ₹500–₹2,500/month and GSTR-3B at ₹500–₹2,000/month, depending on invoice volume.
  - About ₹500–₹1,500/month for a small proprietorship, ₹800–₹2,000 for a partnership, and ₹1,500–₹5,000 for a Pvt Ltd.
  - GSTR-2B reconciliation at about ₹2,000–₹15,000/year.
  - Vendor plans from ₹999/year (turnover ≤ ₹20 lakh) to about ₹6,000–₹9,899/year.
  — [RegisterKaro – cost of hiring CA](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/cost-of-hiring-ca-in-india); [TAXAJ – 2026 compliance cost](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=766); [Kanakkupillai – GST return filing charges](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-return-filing-charges/); [QwikFilings](https://qwikfilings.com/gst-return-filing/)
- **QRMP** is available for turnover up to ₹5 crore and reduces annual returns from up to 24 to 8. — [AI Accountant – GST filing guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/gst-filing-india-guide)
- Statutory late fees: sources conflict. One says GSTR-1 is ₹50/day up to ₹5,000 and GSTR-3B is ₹20/day up to ₹10,000. Another says ₹50/day capped at ₹10,000 per return. — [TAXAJ – late fee calculator](https://www.taxaj.com/gst-late-fees-calculator); [AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/gst-filing-india-guide)

### Inferences
- The statutory basis for the dropshipper's ITC is the Explanation to Section 16(2)(b) of the CGST Act. It deems goods delivered to a third party on the recipient's direction to be received by the recipient. This is from general knowledge, not verified in this session. Other ITC conditions, also from general knowledge: a valid tax invoice, the credit reflecting in GSTR-2B (Section 16(2)(aa)), tax actually paid by the supplier, and the store's own return filed. **Practical rule: only use GST-registered suppliers who invoice the store's GSTIN with the customer's address as ship-to.**
- Standard return calendar, from general knowledge and to be verified on gst.gov.in:
  - Monthly filers: GSTR-1 by the 11th and GSTR-3B by the 20th of the next month.
  - QRMP filers: quarterly GSTR-1 by the 13th and GSTR-3B by the 22nd or 24th (by state) after the quarter, with monthly tax paid via PMT-06.
  - Annual GSTR-9 is optional for turnover up to ₹2 crore.
- An e-way bill is generally required for consignments above ₹50,000. In dropshipping the supplier's movement usually triggers it, so it is rarely relevant to small B2C parcels (general knowledge; confirm with CA).
- **Budget guide:** ₹0 government fee for registration; about ₹6,000–₹18,000/year in CA fees for a low-volume B2C store, less under QRMP and more with high invoice volumes or reconciliation work.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (non-vendor) survey of CA fees exists; get 2–3 local quotes.
- I could not verify the exact current late-fee caps or due dates from CBIC sources.

## Q5. Practical reality: do payment gateways (Razorpay, Cashfree) and shipping aggregators require a GSTIN? Can a sole proprietor onboard with PAN + bank account only?

### Takeaway
**Not always.** Cashfree states that GSTIN is **optional** and lets a person without a business PAN register as an **individual** using a personal PAN. Razorpay's proprietorship list accepts a **"GST certificate *or* non-enrollment declaration"**, but also asks for business-registration proof and proof of address. Shiprocket offers **Aadhaar-OTP KYC** for individuals, with GSTIN as one option, not a requirement. Even so, the store needs GST anyway because it sells inter-state (Q1). So gateway flexibility does not remove the legal requirement.

### Cited Findings
- **Razorpay (payment gateway):** the proprietorship document list includes "GST certificate or non-enrollment declaration", plus PAN, government ID, business registration proof, a cancelled cheque and proof of business address. — [Razorpay – documents required for payment gateway](https://razorpay.com/blog/documents-required-for-payment-gateway); [Razorpay – KYC onboarding](https://razorpay.com/blog/payment-gateway-kyc-onboarding-india/)
- **RazorpayX current account (RBL/YES Bank), a different product:** accepted entity proofs include a registration certificate under any statute, a Shop & Establishment certificate, a Gram Panchayat certificate, or a complete ITR in the proprietor's name. "Udyog Aadhaar Memorandum and Online Labour Certificate will not be accepted as entity proof." — [RazorpayX – sole proprietorship KYC](https://razorpay.com/docs/x/rbl-ybl-current-account/sole-proprietorship/kyc.md)
- **Cashfree:** GSTIN is not required for activation, but adding it unlocks GST-related benefits. PAN is mandatory, and someone without a business PAN can register as an Individual with a personal PAN. Owner verification is by Aadhaar OTP, DigiLocker or CKYC. — [Cashfree – onboarding FAQs](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/help/onboarding-related/onboarding-faqs); [Cashfree – account activation](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/help/account/account-activation.md)
- **Shiprocket:** individuals can complete KYC by automated Aadhaar OTP, or manually with Aadhaar or PAN. Sole proprietors can verify via Aadhaar OTP or GSTIN OTP, or manually with PAN or a GST certificate. — [Shiprocket support – KYC documents](https://support.shiprocket.in/support/solutions/articles/43000662578-shiprocket-what-documents-are-required-for-kyc-verification-)
- **Contrary claim:** one GST consultancy article says Indian payment gateways require a GSTIN to settle funds even for an own website. That is a gateway policy, not law, and it contradicts the official Razorpay and Cashfree documents above. — [Disytax – GST for online sellers](https://disytax.com/gst-for-online-sellers/)

### Inferences
- A sole proprietor **can** usually start gateway and shipping onboarding with PAN, Aadhaar and a bank account, and add a GSTIN later. Razorpay's proprietorship route will still want some business proof, such as Shop & Establishment or a GST certificate.
- Because GD Home Fabric presumably already holds a GSTIN, onboarding under that GSTIN is the smoothest route if the owner sells under the same PAN or entity (see Q6).

### Gaps
- Current Razorpay and Shopify Payments India onboarding requirements could not be fetched directly. Policies change, so confirm with each provider. Bank-account verification rules for individual (non-business) Cashfree accounts were not confirmed.

## Q6. Business structure options and costs (sole proprietorship + Udyam + Shop & Establishment, OPC, LLP, Pvt Ltd), and whether the owner can sell under the existing GD Home Fabric entity/GSTIN

### Takeaway
For a small dropshipping test, the cheapest route is a **sole proprietorship**, or running the store **under GD Home Fabric's existing PAN and GSTIN**. A proprietorship has no incorporation cost; Udyam is free, and Shop & Establishment is state-specific and usually low-cost. An **LLP** (about ₹5k–₹15k to set up, ₹11k–₹33k/year below ₹40 lakh turnover) is the cheapest limited-liability option. A **Pvt Ltd** costs about ₹7k–₹25k to set up and about ₹31k–₹92k+/year to maintain, because a statutory audit is mandatory regardless of turnover. GST is **PAN-based per state**, so a new store under the same PAN shares the existing GSTIN and **its turnover adds to GD Home Fabric's** for thresholds and income tax.

### Cited Findings
**Proprietorship add-ons**
- **Udyam (MSME) registration is "Free of Cost and paperless"** on udyamregistration.gov.in. The portal warns "Beware of Fake Sites", and a state government warned that private agents were charging for it. — [Udyam portal](https://www.udyamregistration.gov.in/); [EastMojo – Nagaland warning](https://eastmojo.com/news/2025/06/25/nagaland-warns-against-paying-for-pm-vishwakarma-udyam-registrations/)
- **Shop & Establishment (example: Maharashtra "Gumasta"):** one guide gives fees from **nil** for a proprietor with no employees, through ₹300 for 1–5 employees, up to ₹2,400 for 20+ employees. Another gives ₹1,640 for 1–5 employees (possibly a different municipal schedule). Home-based businesses, including "small online sellers", can register using a residential address. Registration is due within 30 days of starting, with longer or lifetime validity for small establishments. — [RegisterKaro – Gumasta](https://registerkaro.in/gumasta-license); [StartupGrants India – Gumasta](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/services/gumasta-license-maharashtra)

**Company / LLP / OPC set-up costs (estimates)**
- Pvt Ltd all-in: about **₹7,000–₹25,000** for two directors. SPICe+ filing fee is nil for authorised capital up to ₹15 lakh. Digital signature certificates (DSC) cost about ₹800–₹2,500 per director. ## Q9. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, and Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023: obligations for a seller with its own website

### Takeaway
A dropshipping store selling in its own name on its own site is an **"e-commerce entity"**, most plausibly an **inventory e-commerce entity**. It must display:
- its legal name, geographic address, and customer care and **grievance officer** details;
- return, refund, exchange, warranty, delivery and payment policies;
- the total price with breakup, and country of origin.

It must acknowledge complaints within **48 hours** and resolve them within **one month**. Under Legal Metrology Rule 6(10), product pages must show the package declarations: manufacturer/packer/importer name and address, country of origin, generic name, net quantity, MRP, unit sale price, size, and consumer-care details. **New from 1 Jul 2026:** listings of **imported** goods must have a searchable/sortable **country-of-origin filter** (Rule 6(10A)). The CCPA dark-patterns guidelines prohibit fake countdown timers and false scarcity, drip pricing and basket sneaking, among others.

### Cited Findings
**E-Commerce Rules 2020**
- Every e-commerce entity must display its legal name, the principal geographic address of its HQ and branches, website details, and customer care and grievance officer contact details. — [ONDC legal compliance handbook – E-Commerce Rules](https://ondc.org/legal-compliance-handbook/ANNEXURE-%E2%80%93-E.1086291982.html)
- It must appoint a grievance officer and publish their name, contact details and designation (Rule 4(4)). The officer acknowledges complaints within **48 hours** and redresses them within **one month** (Rule 4(5)). The rules also cover foreign entities that systematically offer goods to Indian consumers. — [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/news/e-commerce-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020-notified); [example Shopify store grievance policy citing Rule 4(4)/(5)](https://beyondappliances.in/pages/grievance-redressal-policy)
- Inventory e-commerce entities "own the inventory … and sell directly to consumers", including single-brand retailers. They must publish policies on returns, refunds, exchange, warranty and guarantee, delivery and shipment, payment modes and grievance redressal, and **display the total price**. Their disclosure duties mirror those of sellers, except for some detailed product information. — [Khaitan & Co (PDF)](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2020-08/Stricter%20Regulations%20on%20E-Commerce%20%E2%80%93%20The%20Consumer%20Protection%20(E-Commerce)%20Rules,%202020.pdf); [Desi Kaanoon](https://desikaanoon.in/analysis-of-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020)
- Sellers must give the total price in figures with a breakup, the **country of origin**, grievance officer details, warranties and guarantees, and refund, exchange and return-shipping information. Goods that are defective, deficient or delivered late cannot be refused for return (force majeure excepted). Inventory entities bear liability for **authenticity** claims they vouch for. — [Mondaq – E-Commerce Rules 2020](https://www.mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/976876/e-commerce-rules-2020); [IndiaLaw summary](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- Rule 4(9) requires explicit and affirmative consent (no pre-ticked boxes). The CCPA reminded businesses of this in June 2025. — [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/consumer/dark-patterns-in-the-digital-economy/)

**Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011**
- Rule 6(10): every e-commerce entity shall display the Rule 6(1) declarations on its digital network. These include the manufacturer/packer/importer name and address, country of origin, common/generic name, net quantity, best-before (where applicable), MRP, dimensions, and consumer-care details. The **month/year of manufacture is excluded** from the online display (2017 amendment, notified 23 Jun 2017). — [S.S. Rana – Legal Metrology for e-commerce](https://ssrana.in/articles/compliances-for-website-e-commerce-website/); [Mondaq – compliances for e-commerce website](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1532648/compliances-for-websitee-commerce-website); [Delhi HC PIL reproducing amended rule](https://livelaw.in/pdf_upload/pil-delhi-hc-388086.pdf)
- **Unit sale price** (per kg, litre or unit) is reported as mandatory since 2022 (secondary source). — [CalcGuru – LMPC 2026](https://calcguru.in/?p=4506)
- E-tailers can be fined up to ₹1 lakh for failing to display country of origin (2020 reporting). — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/money/e-tailers-can-be-fined-rs-1-lakh-failing-display-country-origin-128355)
- **New 2026 change:** the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Amendment Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 128(E), 13 Feb 2026) insert **Rule 6(10A)**. Every e-commerce entity selling **imported** products must offer listings "in a searchable and sortable filter specifying the country of origin". **In force from 1 Jul 2026.** — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/21/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2026-explained/); [S.S. Rana – LMPC amended](https://ssrana.in/articles/lmpc-rules-amended-new-compliance-for-e-commerce/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/38270-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2026-enter-into-force)
- One law-firm article refers to further amendments notified in **April 2026** with enhanced disclosure obligations, but their content could not be confirmed. — [Mondaq – Khurana & Khurana](https://www.mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1806934/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2026-enhancing-transparency-and-consumer-protection-in-e-commerce)

**Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023 (CCPA)**
- Notified **30 Nov 2023**. They prohibit the dark patterns listed in Annexure I (non-exhaustive), including:
  - **False urgency:** "falsely stating or implying the sense of urgency or scarcity".
  - **Drip pricing:** price elements "not revealed upfront or … revealed surreptitiously".
  - **Basket sneaking:** necessary fees disclosed at purchase are not basket sneaking.
  — [Bar & Bench – CCPA steps in](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/ccpa-steps-in-to-curb-dark-patterns); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/consumer/dark-patterns-in-the-digital-economy/); [Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/ccpa-new-guidelines-to-target-dark-patterns-like-subscription-trapping-nagging-131246.html)
- **5 Jun 2025 advisory:** e-commerce platforms were told to **self-audit within three months** and to self-declare. By Nov 2025, 26 platforms had submitted declarations (single source). The CCPA has penalised 7 platforms (about ₹20 lakh in total) and treated a **recurring 24-hour countdown timer** as false urgency. — [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/dark-patterns-back-in-spotlight-as-new-advisory-calls-for-self-audits/); [Bar & Bench – from nudge to notice](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/law-firms/view-point/from-nudge-to-notice-what-indias-dark-pattern-orders-mean-for-digital-business)

### Inferences
**Shopify store checklist (required)**
- Footer, contact and policy pages with:
  - the legal name (the proprietor's name or entity name, plus "trading as" the brand);
  - the full address;
  - customer-care email and phone;
  - grievance officer name, designation and contact, with the 48-hour / 1-month commitment.
- Published policies: return/refund/exchange, shipping/delivery, cancellation, payment methods, warranty, terms and privacy.
- Every product page shows:
  - the all-inclusive MRP and the selling price;
  - net quantity or count and size or dimensions;
  - the manufacturer, packer or importer name and address (the supplier's details);
  - country of origin and consumer-care details.
- Checkout shows the full price breakup (shipping and COD fees) **before** payment.

**Avoid these common Shopify apps and patterns**
- Fake or looping countdown timers.
- Fake "only 2 left" or "23 people viewing" widgets.
- Pre-ticked "shipping protection" add-ons.
- COD or "convenience" fees revealed only at the last step.
- Fake reviews.

**Also note**
- Dropshipping does not shift these duties to the supplier. To the consumer, the store is the seller. It is also likely a "product seller" for product-liability purposes under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 (general knowledge; confirm with a lawyer).
- If the store re-packs or private-labels products, it may need **LMPC registration as a packer/importer** under Legal Metrology. Reselling the supplier's labelled packages generally does not (general knowledge; confirm).

## Q10. Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: what a small store must do, and when

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified on **13 Nov 2025** (G.S.R. 846(E)) with a **phased rollout**. Data Protection Board provisions applied immediately, consent-manager provisions apply from about **Nov 2026**, and the substantive duties of data fiduciaries apply from about **13 May 2027**. Those duties are a clear privacy notice, valid consent, security safeguards, breach notification, rights handling and retention limits. In Jan–Feb 2026 MeitY **proposed** shortening the timeline to 12 months (about 13 Nov 2026), mainly for large or "significant" data fiduciaries. I found no final notification of that change. A small Shopify store is a "data fiduciary" and should build compliance in now.

### Cited Findings
- The rules were notified 13 Nov 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)). Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 (Data Protection Board) are in force immediately. Rule 4 (consent managers) applies after about 12 months. Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 apply after **18 months**. Those rules cover data fiduciary obligations, notice and consent, data principal rights, **reasonable security safeguards**, children's data, breach notification and cross-border transfers. — [S.S. Rana – DPDP Rules 2025](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/); [TCSA – implementation roadmap](https://www.tcsa.in/resources/dpdp-rules-2025-implementation-roadmap); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-)
- The final deadline is reported as **12 or 13 May 2027** (sources differ by a day). Breach reporting to the Board is within **72 hours**. Penalties run up to **₹250 crore** (secondary). — [Mondaq – DPDP compliance mandate](https://www.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1708830/dpdp-act-compliance-mandate); [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/blog/current-affairs/dpdp-rules-2025-framework-for-personal-data-protection)
- **Proposed compression:** in late Jan 2026, MeitY sought industry views by 4 Feb 2026 on cutting the 18-month period to **12 months**, which would move full compliance to 13 Nov 2026. Reports say this targets **Significant Data Fiduciaries** (large tech, BFSI). The government signalled the intent on 17 Nov 2025. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm); [S.S. Rana – MeitY plans to cut short timeline](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [GoTrust](https://www.gotrust.tech/newsletter/meity-may-cut-dpdp-compliance-timeline-from-18-to-12-months)

### Inferences
**Minimum DPDP to-do for a small store (build before May 2027, ideally before Nov 2026)**
- A standalone privacy notice in plain language (English and, ideally, Hindi or the regional language). It should itemise the data collected (name, phone, address, email, payment metadata) and the purposes (order fulfilment, delivery, support, marketing), say how to withdraw consent, explain how to exercise rights, give the grievance or contact person, and mention the right to complain to the Data Protection Board.
- Separate, unticked opt-in consent for marketing (WhatsApp, SMS, email), as distinct from data needed to fulfil the order.
- Data-processing agreements, or at least awareness of the terms, with processors: Shopify, the payment gateway, Shiprocket or the courier, the dropship supplier who receives customer addresses, and marketing tools.
- Basic security: two-factor login on Shopify, staff accounts with least privilege, no customer data in spreadsheets shared over WhatsApp.
- A breach-response plan: notify affected users and the Board (72 hours for the detailed report).
- A deletion and retention policy.

**Before the DPDP duties bite:** the E-Commerce Rules' explicit-consent requirement (Rule 4(9)) already applies. The IT Act's SPDI rules (Section 43A) also continue to apply until replaced (general knowledge; confirm).

## Q11. Income tax: Section 44AD presumptive taxation, ITR forms, tax audit thresholds, TDS/TCS (194-O, 206C(1H)), and the Income-tax Act 2025

### Takeaway
A proprietor (or non-LLP partnership firm) can use **presumptive taxation**: deemed profit is **8% of turnover**, or **6% of digital receipts**. Turnover limits are **₹2 crore**, or **₹3 crore if cash receipts are ≤5%**. This avoids detailed books and audit, and nearly all of a Shopify store's receipts are digital, so the 6% rate fits. Tax audit applies above ₹1 crore turnover (₹10 crore if cash ≤5%), or if the owner opts out of 44AD within five years and declares lower profit. **Section 194-O TDS does not apply to sales on the store's own site.** It applies when an e-commerce operator facilitates sales by participants, such as Amazon or Flipkart. **From 1 Apr 2026** the **Income-tax Act 2025** replaces the 1961 Act: 44AD becomes **Section 58** and 194-O becomes **Section 393(1)**, with "tax year" terminology.

### Cited Findings
- 44AD: deemed income 8% of turnover, or 6% of digital receipts. The limit is ₹3 crore for FY 2025-26 if cash receipts are ≤5% (95% digital), otherwise ₹2 crore. It is available to resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms (not LLPs or companies). No detailed books or audit are needed if 6–8% or more is declared. — [Tax2win – 44AD](https://tax2win.in/guide/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme); [Vyapar – 44AD](https://vyaparapp.in/blog/section-44ad-presumptive-taxation/); [Skydo – 44AD](https://www.skydo.com/blog/44ad-of-income-tax-act)
- The **Income-tax Act 2025** is notified to come into force **1 Apr 2026** (Tax Year 2026-27). **Section 58** consolidates 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE. Business rates stay at 8% / 6% with the ₹2 crore / ₹3 crore limits. Eligibility shifts to an "assessee-wise disqualification" test. Commentators say Section 58 bars set-off of losses and deductions, and that the audit trigger becomes an income-outcome test. — [TaxGuru – presumptive taxation 1961 vs 2025 Act](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/presumptive-taxation-residents-1961-act-via-a-vis-2025-act.html); [Accountune – Section 58](https://accountune.com/resumptive-taxation-for-small-business); [ICAI NIRC – New Income Tax Act 2025 (PDF)](https://nirc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/The-New-Income-Tax-Act-2025-nanda-sir.pdf); [TaxGuru – business income under 2025 Act](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/income-business-income-tax-act-2025-sections-itr-forms-compliance-ay-2026-27.html)
- **Tax audit (44AB, 1961 Act):** ₹1 crore turnover, rising to ₹10 crore if cash receipts and payments are each ≤5%. Audit is also required where a taxpayer who opted into 44AD opts out within five years and income exceeds the basic exemption limit. The penalty under 271B is 0.5% of turnover or ₹1.5 lakh, whichever is lower. Under the 2025 Act, audit sits in **Section 63**. One source claims a ₹15 crore limit for 95%-digital businesses (unconfirmed outlier). — [TaxGuru – 44AB criteria](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/audit-criteria-business-profession-section-44ab-ay-2024-25.html); [Treelife](https://treelife.in/?p=16039); [CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=8192); [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/about-section-44ab-of-income-tax-act)
- **Section 194-O:** the e-commerce operator deducts TDS where it facilitates sales by an "e-commerce participant" through its platform. The rate is **0.1% from 1 Oct 2024** (earlier 1%). The CBDT issued Q&A guidance (Circular 20/2023). The provision moves to **Section 393(1)** of the 2025 Act from 1 Apr 2026. — [Income Tax Department – 194-O](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-194-o-6); [Tax2win – 194-O](https://tax2win.in/guide/section-194o-of-the-income-tax-act); [TDSMAN – Section 393(1)](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/07/tds-on-e-commerce-transactions-section-3931-194o/); [Deloitte – CBDT guidelines](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/in/Documents/tax/in-tax-gbt-guidelines-issued-for-TDS-on-payment-noexp.pdf)
- **TCS on sale of goods (206C(1H))** is reported **omitted from 1 Apr 2025** by Finance Act 2025. This comes from a weak secondary source and should be confirmed. — [Scribd TCS compilation](https://www.scribd.com/document/877993459/TCS)

### Inferences
- **Own-website store:** 194-O does not apply. Shopify does not facilitate or collect consideration, and the store is not a participant on someone else's platform. If the owner also sells on Amazon or Flipkart, 0.1% TDS (194-O / 393(1)) and GST TCS apply to those sales. They are creditable in the ITR and GST ledger.
- **Which law applies when:** FY 2025-26 income (filed in 2026) is under the 1961 Act (44AD). Income from 1 Apr 2026 onward is under the 2025 Act (Section 58).
- **ITR form:** typically **ITR-4 (Sugam)** for presumptive income of individuals, HUFs and firms, subject to an income cap; **ITR-3** if regular books are kept. This is from general knowledge, and the forms for Tax Year 2026-27 under the new Act should be confirmed.
- **Advance tax:** a 44AD taxpayer can pay all advance tax by 15 March (general knowledge; confirm).
- **Combined turnover:** if run under GD Home Fabric's proprietorship, the dropshipping turnover is added to GD Home Fabric's turnover for 44AD limits and audit thresholds.
- **Caution:** dropshipping margins are thin, but 44AD deems 6% of turnover as profit. If actual profit is lower, declaring less may trigger books plus audit, especially under the new Section 58/63 rules. This is a key point to confirm with a CA.

## Q12. Consolidated checklist (required vs optional), cost summary, and answers to the most common confusion points

### Takeaway
For a Shopify dropshipping store shipping across India, the **legally required** items are:
- GST registration (₹0 government fee) and monthly or quarterly returns;
- a PAN-linked bank account for settlements;
- E-Commerce Rules disclosures and a grievance officer;
- Legal Metrology declarations on every listing;
- no dark patterns;
- a privacy notice and consent design (DPDP binding by May 2027);
- income-tax filing.

Shop & Establishment registration is required in many states once trading starts (state-specific).

**Optional / strategic:** Udyam (free; unlocks MSME benefits and the lower trademark fee for entities), a trademark, an LLP or Pvt Ltd, and DPIIT recognition.

**Indicative first-year cost (proprietorship route):**
- About ₹0–₹3,000 in government fees, excluding trademark.
- About ₹6,000–₹18,000 in CA fees for GST, plus ITR fees (a gap).
- Optionally ₹13,500 in government fees plus about ₹6,000–₹30,000 for an attorney for a 3-class trademark.

The LLP route adds about ₹5k–₹15k to set up and ₹11k–₹33k/year. The Pvt Ltd route adds about ₹7k–₹25k to set up and ₹31k–₹92k+/year.

### Inferences
**Common confusion points, answered**
1. **"I only sell on my own Shopify site, so I don't need GST until ₹40 lakh."** This is true only if every sale is to customers in the owner's own state. One sale to another state requires registration (Section 24(i)).
2. **"The 2023 rule lets small sellers skip GST."** It applies only to sellers on **marketplaces** (ECOs), **within one state**, with an enrolment number. It does not cover own-website sellers or inter-state sales.
3. **"Shopify is an e-commerce operator, so it collects TCS / 194-O TDS."** No. Shopify is a software (SaaS) provider and the store collects its own money, so neither GST TCS nor 194-O applies. These apply only if the store also sells on Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho.
4. **"Composition at 1% is cheapest."** It is not allowed with inter-state sales, it allows no ITC, and the store cannot charge GST to customers. It is unsuitable for a pan-India store.
5. **"Razorpay or Cashfree need a GSTIN."** Not strictly: a non-enrolment declaration or individual PAN onboarding exists. But the store needs GST anyway for inter-state sales.
6. **"The supplier ships, so the supplier handles compliance."** No. The store is the seller of record for GST invoices, the E-Commerce Rules, Legal Metrology display, returns and refunds, product liability, and DPDP.
7. **"Can I use GD Home Fabric's GSTIN?"** Yes, if the store runs under the same PAN (same proprietor or entity) in the same state. Amend core fields (additional place of business; trade name if needed) via REG-14. Turnover is combined for GST and 44AD. A separate LLP or company is needed only for liability or brand separation.
8. **"44AD means I pay tax on 6% regardless."** The 6% is a floor. Declaring less is allowed only with books, and possibly an audit. Dropshipping margins should be compared with 6% of turnover.
9. **"GST on bedsheets is 5%."** Mostly yes for pieces at or below ₹2,500 after 22 Sep 2025. Above ₹2,500 per piece, 18% is likely (sources conflict). Verify by HSN.
10. **"DPDP doesn't apply until 2027."** The substantive duties are phased, but the consent rules under the E-Commerce Rules already apply, and MeitY has proposed bringing the deadline forward. Build the privacy notice and consent flows now.

**Points the owner should confirm with a CA:** GD Home Fabric's constitution and state; GSTIN amendment vs a new entity; HSN-wise rates (Chapter 63 above ₹2,500, Chapter 94); 44AD vs books given dropshipping margins; and the ITR form under the 2025 Act.
