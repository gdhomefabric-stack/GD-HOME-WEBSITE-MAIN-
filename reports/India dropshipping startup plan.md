# Launch a Prepaid-First Home Furnishings Store

The best way for you to start dropshipping in India in October 2026 is a separately branded home-furnishings store selling coordinated bedroom and living-room bundles at ₹1,299–₹3,999, run as a hybrid: GD Home Fabric's own curtains and pillows as the hero products, one or two vetted small-batch makers for matching pieces, and a low-fee catalog supplier (DeoDap, about ₹5,900 a year) for add-ons. Pure app-based dropshipping is weaker than it looks, because Roposo Clout, the default Indian Shopify dropship app, shut on 1 April 2026, China-based suppliers cannot offer COD (cash on delivery, which most Indian shoppers expect), and every remaining catalog supplier carries complaints about delays and missing items. Budget about **₹77,000–₹1.02 lakh** for a lean four-month test, or **₹1.8–₹2.6 lakh** for the recommended plan plus up to ₹1.1 lakh more in ads that you release only if a bundle proves profitable; after launch, fixed running costs are about **₹3,300–₹4,300 a month** (lean) or **₹8,200–₹11,900 a month** (recommended) before ads and per-order costs. The number that decides profit is RTO (return to origin: the buyer refuses the parcel or can't be reached, so it travels back and you pay shipping both ways for no sale), which hits roughly one COD order in four. In the worked example below, a ₹1,499 bundle can spend about **₹608 on ads per order** and still break even (its break-even CPA, or cost per acquisition — the ad spend needed to win one order), equal to a break-even ROAS (return on ad spend: sales divided by ad spend) of about 2.5x, while a ₹599 product can afford only about ₹153 — far below what Meta ads typically cost per order in India. Legally, a store that ships to other states needs a GSTIN (GST registration number) from its first out-of-state sale whatever its turnover, and you can probably use GD Home Fabric's existing GSTIN if the store runs under the same PAN. Diwali falls around 8 November and festive ad prices run 30–50% higher, so spend the next four weeks setting up and sourcing, and start paid testing in mid-November. Every price here comes from search-result snapshots of vendor pages taken on 13 October 2026, so re-check each one on the official page before you pay, and treat the legal and tax points as general information to confirm with your CA.

## Home-furnishing bundles suit you better than gadgets or fashion

Most "best niches for India 2026" lists come from suppliers selling to dropshippers and name the same handful of categories — kitchen gadgets, pet, car accessories, fitness and festive gifting — with little data behind them ([CIIM](https://www.ciim.in/10-most-profitable-dropshipping-niches-product-categories-in-india-2026/); [Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/blog/dropshipping-products-to-sell-in-india/)). The harder evidence concerns return risk and basket size, and it points to home furnishing. Vendor benchmarks put RTO for **home décor and furnishings at roughly 10–28%**, against **25–40% for fashion** (up to 35–45% on COD orders), 8–18% for electronics and 4–12% for jewellery ([Base](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/rto-rates-in-india/)). Home orders are larger too, with a "good" average order value (AOV, the typical basket) of ₹1,500–₹4,000, although that benchmark cites no underlying data ([MakeMeConvert](https://www.makemeconvert.com/blog/indian-d2c-conversion-rate-benchmarks)). The market is large and open: Redseer sizes Indian home décor at about **US$2 billion, growing over 10% a year and highly fragmented** ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/indias-2b-home-decor-market-a-fragmented-growth-opportunity/)); within home textiles, bed linen is the largest segment and **curtains are the fastest-growing** ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/home-textile-market/india)); and the November–December wedding season adds gifting and home-refresh demand, with CAIT projecting **46 lakh weddings and ₹6.5 lakh crore of spending** for the 2025 season ([CAIT](https://cait.in/wedding-season-2025-to-generate-₹6-5-lakh-crore-business-from-46-lakh-weddings-across-india-cait-delhi-alone-to-witness-₹1-8-lakh-crore-trade-from-4-8-lakh-weddings-indian/)). Your curtain and pillow supply is an advantage almost no new dropshipper has: you control quality, you can make to measure, and you can hold stock of whatever sells.

The category has real weaknesses, and the plan has to route around them. Commodity items are anchored at rock-bottom prices — velvet cushion-cover 5-packs appear in Amazon India deals at ₹253–₹292 ([DealsMagnet](https://www.dealsmagnet.com/deal/aerohaven-velvet-250tc-cushion-cover-16-x-16-inch--1biQZkTf); [DealsMagnet](https://www.dealsmagnet.com/deal/stitchnest-both-side-quilted-velvet-cushion-cover-19zDl9zJ)) and Kuber Industries sets at ₹1,199–₹2,299 ([GlobalBees](https://shop.globalbees.com/products/kuber-industries-self-design-5-piece-jute-cushion-2000000086384)) — so a plain cushion cover cannot carry a ₹600 ad cost. Home and living converts slightly below average, at about **1.4% of visitors** against a 1.6% blended D2C rate ([Cognito IT](https://cognitoitconsultancy.com/ecommerce-conversion-rate/)); curtains and bedding are bulky to ship (see the shipping section); and big rivals spend heavily, with IKEA India putting ₹223.9 crore into advertising and promotion in FY25 ([Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/news/retail-india-news-ikea-india-loss-widens-rs-13252-cr-fy25)). The answer is to sell what marketplaces handle badly: room-coordinated bundles (a curtain pair plus cushion covers plus a throw or table runner) at ₹1,499–₹3,999, made-to-measure or premium fabrics such as linen, velvet and blackout, regional craft prints such as block print and Kalamkari, and festive or wedding gift sets — with swatch kits sold prepaid, so hesitant buyers commit a small payment before placing a full COD order.

Build one branded niche store — a "bedroom and living" furnishing brand, for example — rather than a general store or a one-product store. Niche stores build the trust and repeat purchase that matter doubly in a COD market, where buyers judge whether a store is credible before they pay ([DoDropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/general-vs-niche-dropshipping-store/); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=50153)), and you should launch with 10–20 products rather than 100, since a bloated catalogue is a named beginner mistake ([SeekhoBecho](https://www.seekhobecho.com/blog/why-dropshipping-fails-in-india/)). For the web address, a free subdomain such as shop.gdhomefabric.in connects to Shopify with a single DNS record (a CNAME pointing at shops.myshopify.com) ([Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/domains/add-a-domain/connecting-domains/connect-subdomain)), but it ties GD Home Fabric's name to third-party products, COD disputes and any email-spam flags; a new .in domain costs ₹1–₹599 in its first year and about ₹800–₹900 a year afterwards ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/in/tld/in-domain); [Codeshoppy](https://codeshoppy.com/blog/domain-hosting-costs-india-2026/)). **My recommendation is a separate brand on its own .in domain, with GD Home Fabric as the legal seller behind it** — cheap insurance for the business you already have.

If you would rather sell something unrelated to textiles, pet supplies and car accessories fit next best on price and return risk, though their case rests on supplier blogs rather than data ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/blog/dropshipping-products-to-sell-in-india/)). Avoid fashion, for its return rates and Meesho's ₹399–₹499 price anchors; avoid stand-alone kitchen gadgets, which now compete with Amazon's Low-Price Store for items under ₹600 ([DigitalDawn](https://www.digitaldawn.in/amazon-vs-flipkart-vs-meesho-best-platform-indian-sellers-2025/); [EcomShadow](https://www.ecomshadow.com/blog/meesho-vs-amazon-india-2026-seller-comparison-1775576971197)); and avoid electronics that need BIS or WPC certification, along with baby products and their safety risk.

## Roposo's shutdown makes a hybrid supply chain the safer bet

The Indian dropship-supplier market changed sharply in 2026. Roposo Clout shut its marketplace **on 1 April 2026**, permanently ending order placement, supplier listings and invoice generation, and told sellers to keep their own tax records because invoices can no longer be reissued ([Roposo Clout notice](https://app.roposoclout.com/)). GlowRoad, owned by Amazon, has an unclear status, and a competitor's guide updated in June 2026 says its Shopify app is no longer listed ([Qikink](https://qikink.com/blog/glowroad-dropshipping-the-ultimate-guide-for-online-sellers/)). Baapstore is still active, but its own pages list plans from ₹4,999 to ₹31,199 a year ([Baapstore Plan Details](https://www.baapstore.com/Plan-Details); [Baapstore pricing](https://www.baapstore.com/pricing)); a competitor-run review says the plans changed four times in three months ([HeadlessBiz](https://www.headlessbiz.com/baapstore-reviews)), and complaints describe sample orders missing items, products that didn't match photos, and no refunds ([ConsumerComplaints.in](https://www.consumercomplaints.in/bycompany/baapstore-a480724.html)). Meesho is a huge marketplace but has no Shopify integration, so you would place each customer's order by hand ([Qikink](https://qikink.com/blog/meesho-dropshipping/)).

That leaves **DeoDap** (Rajkot; home, kitchen and gadgets) as the lowest-risk catalog option, with real caveats. Its annual plan costs **₹4,999 + GST (₹5,898)** and shipping runs ₹50 per 500 g + GST ([DeoDap](https://deodap.in/blogs/news/what-is-the-true-cost-of-starting-a-dropshipping-business)), and its current FAQ says COD works on all plans ([DeoDap FAQ](https://dropshipping.deodap.com/faqs.php)). Its Shopify app, Wukusy, rates 4.4 from 47 reviews, with complaints about payouts delayed about three weeks and an **unannounced ₹25 COD fee** ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/wukusy-indian-dropshipping/reviews)); Trustpilot reviewers report **15–20-day deliveries against a promised 5–7** ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/deodap.in)); and damage claims need an unboxing video within 48 hours ([DeoDap terms](https://dropshipping.deodap.com/terms-and-conditions.php)).

China is not a workable per-order source for an Indian COD store. AliExpress has been blocked since 2020, and one guide says checkout and shipping to India remain disabled ([AliBestFinds](https://alibestfinds.com/aliexpress-india-shopping-guide/)). India has **no duty-free allowance for low-value commercial parcels** ([Zonos](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/country-guides/india)), and duty plus IGST came to about 42% on top of the item price in one example ([HereWeShip](https://hereweship.com/en/shipping-to-india-from-us-2026-ecommerce-guide/)). CJ's own blog says many dropshippers avoid marketing to India ([CJ Dropshipping](https://cjdropshipping.com/blogs/logistics-&-supply-chain/Dropshipping-Shipping-Strategies-and-Methods)), and its parcels take 7–40 days to arrive ([CourierTrackingz](https://couriertrackingz.com/cj-packet-tracking/)).

India's textile clusters, by contrast, sell in small enough batches for a hybrid model. Their minimum order quantities (MOQ, the smallest batch a maker will sell) are low: Jaipur block-print bedsheets come at MOQs of **5, 10 or 50 pieces at about ₹1,101–₹1,150 each** ([Meera Handicrafts](https://www.meerahandicrafts.in/hand-block-printed-bedsheets.html)), with a king-size sheet at ₹1,320 for MOQ 10 ([Shree Ganesh Handicrafts](https://www.shreeganeshhandicrafts.com/hand-block-print-bedsheet.html)). Panipat curtains run 50–500 pieces ([ExportersIndia](https://www.exportersindia.com/panipat/drapes-curtains.htm)) and cushion covers 100–500 ([TradeIndia](https://www.tradeindia.com/panipat/designer-cushion-cover-city-211134.html)), while Karur printed bedsheets run 30–100 ([ExportersIndia](https://www.exportersindia.com/karur/printed-bed-sheet.htm)). For private label, one Gurugram maker quotes **50 knitted or 100 woven pieces per colourway**, with paid samples credited against the first bulk order ([H&J Exim](https://hjepl.com/private-label)). When you find makers on IndiaMART, remember that its TrustSEAL badge checks documents, not quality, and pay only to the bank account IndiaMART has verified ([IndiaMART](https://export.indiamart.com/trustworthiness/); [IndiaMART Buyer Protection](https://buyer.indiamart.com/payment-protection)).

Put together, the safest supply chain has three layers. The first is GD Home Fabric's own curtains and pillows, packed by you and shipped through a courier aggregator; from the new brand's point of view this is still dropshipping, because the brand holds no stock of its own, but quality and dispatch speed stay in your hands. The second is one or two cluster makers for matching pieces, starting with Jaipur's 5–10-piece lots held in your own stock, and asking any maker willing to blind-ship (send single orders straight to your customers with no supplier branding) to put its terms in writing. The third is DeoDap for low-risk add-on tests only, never for hero products until your own test orders prove its dispatch times. Speed matters because Shipway found COD RTO of **22% when the first delivery attempt came within 1–2 days, rising to 35% after more than 5 days** ([Mediabrief](https://mediabrief.com/shipnotes-reveals-26-rto-rate-on-cod-orders-across-india/)), so a slow supplier directly raises your return rate.

Before you scale any supplier, check its GSTIN on the GST portal and its company age on the MCA portal ([Nventory](https://nventory.io/blog/drop-shipping-suppliers-in-india)), and place 2–5 sample orders, including one COD order to a smaller (Tier-2) city PIN code ([Spocket](https://www.spocket.co/blogs/indiamart-dropshipping)). Time how long each order takes to get a working tracking number, check the parcel for supplier branding or slipped-in invoices ([ecom-store.pro](https://shopify.ecom-store.pro/blog/dropshipping-supplier-vetting-checklist/)), and get dispatch times, return terms and COD fees in writing ([HeadlessBiz](https://www.headlessbiz.com/post/shopify-dropshipping-checklist-what-to-set-up-before-you-launch)). Insist that every supplier is GST-registered and invoices your GSTIN, with "bill to" you and "ship to" the customer; only then can you claim input tax credit (ITC, deducting the GST you paid on purchases from the GST you owe) ([ICMAI](https://icmai.in/TaxationPortal/upload/IDT/Article_GST/47.pdf); [Swipe](https://getswipe.in/blog/post/gst-for-dropshipping-a-guide-for-indian-businesses)).

## Shopify Basic and Razorpay take about 4.7% of each prepaid order

Choose Shopify Basic, at **₹1,499 a month billed yearly or ₹1,994 billed monthly, plus 18% GST**; new stores get a 3-day trial and then **₹20 a month for the first three months** ([Shopify India pricing](https://www.shopify.com/in/pricing); [Avada](https://avada.io/blog/shopify-price-in-india/); [Noir & Blanco](https://noirandblanco.com/shopify-pricing-in-india/)). Including GST, monthly billing works out to ₹2,353 a month and yearly billing to ₹1,769 a month (₹21,226 paid upfront). Once the ₹20 offer is counted, the first-year cost is almost the same either way, so bill monthly while you test and switch to yearly once a product works. You can reclaim the GST if you enter your GSTIN at signup ([PayGlocal](https://payglocal.in/blog/shopify-price-india-guide)). Two other plans are not worth it at launch. Starter (₹399) is built for selling through social-media links, and an older Shopify page lists a 5% fee for it ([Shopify Starter](https://www.shopify.com/in/starter)), so it is not a real storefront option. Grow cuts Shopify's per-order fee from 2% to 1%, but by my arithmetic it pays off only above about **₹4.1 lakh a month of prepaid sales**.

Payments go through an outside gateway, and both the gateway and Shopify take a cut. Shopify Payments does not operate in India, so you must use a third-party gateway such as Razorpay or Cashfree, and on top of the gateway's fee Shopify charges its own **third-party transaction fee of 2% on Basic** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/preciousky_45d956626d31c3/shopify-payments-doesnt-operate-in-india-heres-what-that-actually-costs-you-30lp); [Bybtraction](https://bybtraction.com/shopify-pricing-india-plans-fees-hidden-costs/)). UPI is no cheaper on these gateways: banks charge nothing on UPI, but the gateway adds its ~2% platform fee anyway ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/upi-charges-explained-mdr-vs-platform-fees/)). On a ₹1,000 prepaid order on Basic, the stack therefore costs **₹47.20, or 4.72%** — ₹23.60 for Razorpay and ₹23.60 for Shopify, both including GST — falling to about ₹23.60 during Razorpay's 0% window.

## One COD parcel in four comes back, and you pay both ways

India's COD numbers set the economics of this business. COD makes up about **62.5% of Indian e-commerce orders**, with **COD RTO near 24% against under 3% for prepaid**, climbing to 29% in Tier-2 cities and 37.5% in Tier-3 and smaller towns ([ProfitBox360](https://www.profitbox360.com/research/india/ecommerce/cod-prepaid-rto-rates-india)). Shipway's FY25 data shows 26% COD RTO, ranging from 18% in Vadodara to 35% in Patna ([Mediabrief](https://mediabrief.com/shipnotes-reveals-26-rto-rate-on-cod-orders-across-india/)). Unicommerce's 2026 report, covering 410 million shipments, found D2C RTO **peaked at 39.2% in November 2025**, and brands that fixed their operations brought it down to 21.0% by March 2026 ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)). For a new, unknown brand, plan for **25–30% COD RTO normally and 35–40% in festive months**.

The free tiers of shipping aggregators (single dashboards that book many couriers) are enough to start. **Shiprocket Lite costs ₹0 a month** ([checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/shiprocket/pricing)), with Advanced and Pro plans at ₹499 and ₹799 ([Shiprocket](https://www.shiprocket.in/pricing/)), while NimbusPost lists ₹19–₹25.50 per 500 g and pays out COD cash on a free **T+3 cycle**, three days after delivery ([NimbusPost](https://nimbuspost.com/pricing/)). Those headline rates apply to local zones only. Aggregators price by zone — Zone A is within a city, and Zone D is "rest of India" excluding the North-East and J&K ([Shiprocket](https://support.shiprocket.in/support/solutions/articles/43000664184-explaining-shipping-zones-how-shipment-zones-affect-order-fulfillment-and-rates-)) — so plan on **Zone D freight of ₹38–₹57 per 500 g plus 18% GST** ([shipagent.in](https://www.shipagent.in/rate-calculator); [ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/delhivery-courier-charges)). Add a COD fee of **₹35–₹40 or 1.8–2% of the order value, whichever is higher** ([shipagent.in](https://www.shipagent.in/rate-calculator); [Delhivery rate card](https://www.scribd.com/document/502572765/Delhivery-Rate-Card)), and expect return freight on an RTO to cost **80–100% of the forward charge** ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/delhivery-courier-charges)).

Bulky parcels are billed by size, not weight, and that rule is dangerous for your products. Couriers charge the higher of actual weight and "volumetric weight" — length × breadth × height in centimetres divided by 5,000 ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/delhivery-courier-charges)). A 3 kg pillow in a 60×40×30 cm box bills as **14.4 kg**, but vacuum-compressed into a 40×30×15 cm pack it bills as **3.6 kg** ([ShipPrime](https://shipprime.live/resources/blogs/volumetric-weight-vs-actual-weight)). At a flat ₹57 per 500 g that is roughly ₹1,650 against ₹456 before GST, by my arithmetic; heavy-weight slabs are cheaper in practice, but the gap stays large. Curtains should be folded flat into poly mailers, which cost ₹2–₹6 each plain ([Boxish](https://www.boxish.in/blogs/post/ultimate-guide-to-e-commerce-packaging-in-india)) or about ₹9.26 each custom-printed at 100 pieces ([Kraftix](https://www.kraftixdigital.in/polybag/)).

| Shipping cost per parcel, Zone D, incl. 18% GST and packaging | 500 g parcel (₹699 item) | 1.5 kg parcel (₹1,499 item) |
|---|---|---|
| Prepaid, delivered | ₹50–₹72 | ₹155–₹222 |
| COD, delivered | ₹91–₹119 | ₹196–₹269 |
| COD, returned (RTO), COD fee refunded | ₹95–₹140 | ₹289–₹424 |
| COD, returned, COD fee not refunded | ₹136–₹187 | ₹330–₹471 |
| Shipping cost per *delivered* COD order at 26% RTO | ₹124–₹168 | ₹297–₹418 |
| Shipping cost per *delivered* prepaid order at 2% RTO | ₹52–₹75 | ₹160–₹230 |

The table applies the benchmark rates above, charges the 1.5 kg parcel as three first-slab rates (which overstates heavy parcels), assumes return freight equals forward freight, and uses a 26% COD RTO rate ([Mediabrief](https://mediabrief.com/shipnotes-reveals-26-rto-rate-on-cod-orders-across-india/)). Its lesson is that COD plus RTO more than doubles the shipping cost of each delivered 500 g order compared with prepaid — and the bigger loss, the ad spend that won the returned order, comes on top.

Cutting RTO takes a standard set of habits. Confirm every COD order on WhatsApp within five minutes, because GoKwik reports that response rates fall 40% after 30 minutes ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/rto-reduction-tools)), and cancel orders nobody confirms. Offer a ₹50–₹100 prepaid discount, and use partial COD — a ₹50–₹200 advance paid online ([Marqet](https://marqet.in/resources/reduce-rto-cod-orders)), which an app such as Partialy automates ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/partialy-partial-payment-cod)) — on orders above about ₹1,000 or in high-RTO PIN codes. Capping COD order value also helps: FanCode cut RTO 15.5% in a month partly by capping COD at ₹1,500 ([GoKwik](https://www.gokwik.co/case-studies/fancode-reduced-rto-using-gokwik)). Confirmation tools are cheap next to a ₹100–₹470 RTO loss — Shiprocket Engage lists ₹2.49 per order ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/shiprocket-engage)) and Level $4.99 a month ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/level)) — but be sceptical of vendor claims of 30–45% RTO reductions, which cite no independent data ([BotSense](https://botsense.io/blog/cod-verification-rto-numbers-india/)). A realistic target is to move from about 28% to 20–23% COD RTO.

Don't switch COD off entirely, because it still carries close to half of D2C demand ([OneflowAI](https://oneflowai.in/blog/should-you-disable-cod-the-math)). Bain finds the share of COD buyers paying by UPI at the door rose 40% between 2020 and 2025 ([Bain/Flipkart](https://storage.googleapis.com/flipkart-stories-media/How_India_Shops_Online_2026_060094273e/How_India_Shops_Online_2026_060094273e.pdf)), which suggests buyers want assurance that the goods will arrive more than they want to pay in cash. Nudge them toward prepaid with easy refunds and live tracking rather than with blunt COD fees.

## GST registration is compulsory from your first out-of-state order

You need GST registration from day one. Section 24(i) of the CGST Act requires registration for anyone making **inter-state supplies of goods, whatever their turnover** ([KnowYourGST](https://www.knowyourgst.com/gstlaw/the-central-goods-and-services-tax-act-2017/24-compulsory-registration-in-certain-cases-24/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/applicability-gst-online-business-services.html)). The ₹40 lakh threshold helps only a seller whose customers are all in its own state ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-registration-limits-increased)); the October 2023 relief for small sellers covers only marketplace sellers trading within one state ([Notification 34/2023](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/gst-ct-34-2023.pdf)); and the 1% composition scheme bars inter-state sales ([TAXAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/gst-composition-scheme)). Some taxes do not apply to you: Shopify is software, not a marketplace that collects money for you, so the marketplace taxes (GST TCS and income-tax TDS under 194-O) don't apply to your own store ([GST FAQ](https://referencer.in/GST/Files/GST-FAQ_E-commerce.pdf); [Income Tax Department](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-194-o-6)), though they would if you also sold on Amazon or Flipkart. Registration carries no government fee, and since 1 November 2025 the Rule 14A fast track grants a GSTIN **within 3 working days** ([Taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-news/01-11-2025-gstn-has-issued-an-advisory-for-simplified-gst-registration-scheme/)).

You can likely run the store under GD Home Fabric's registration. GST registration is tied to your PAN in each state, so if GD Home Fabric is a proprietorship or firm under the same PAN in the same state, the new store can use the same GSTIN under a separate brand name. You would add a trade name or extra place of business through an amendment (form REG-14), which the officer approves within 15 working days ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-registration-amendment-guide/)); the trade-off is that both businesses' turnover is combined for GST and income tax. A separate legal entity buys liability ring-fencing at a price: an LLP costs ₹5,000–₹15,000 to set up and ₹11,000–₹33,000 a year to maintain, and a private limited company ₹7,000–₹25,000 to set up and ₹31,000–₹92,000+ a year, because a company audit is mandatory ([IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/annual-compliance-cost-pvt-ltd-vs-llp-vs-opc); [TAXAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/company-registration-fees-india/)). Start under the existing set-up and revisit once revenue justifies the extra cost, after confirming GD Home Fabric's legal form and state with your CA.

The GST rate on your products is partly unsettled, so verify it before you set prices. "GST 2.0" moved most goods to **5% and 18% slabs from 22 September 2025** ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/chairman-matexil-welcomes-rectification-of-inverted-duty-structure-for-mmf-textiles)), and for textiles the 5% band now reaches **₹2,500 per piece** ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/impact-rate-rationalization-gst-2-0-regime-textile-sector.html)). Above ₹2,500 the sources conflict. Two say "other made-up textile articles" above ₹2,500 per piece moved to **18%** ([A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gst-rate-rationalisation-on-readymade-garments-to-boost-affordability-and-employment/); [Free Press Journal/PTI](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/garments-above-2500-now-attract-18-gst-industry-warns-of-higher-prices-and-middle-class-impact)); one HSN guide says all of Chapter 63 (bed linen, curtains, cushion covers) now carries a **uniform 5%** ([Disytax](https://disytax.com/?p=19268)); and older tariff pages still show 12% ([IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/hsn-codes/heading-6302)). The rate for **filled pillows and cushions (HSN 9404)** — which matters for your goose-feather pillows — could not be confirmed at all, with a listed history running from 5% to 28% ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/mattress-supports-gst-rates-hsn-code-9404)). The difference is real money: on a single ₹2,999 piece, 5% GST leaves ₹2,856 of revenue and 18% leaves ₹2,542, a ₹314 swing per order that equals half a typical ad cost. Ask your CA to confirm each HSN code (the product's tax code) on the CBIC rate finder against Notification 9/2025-CT(Rate) as amended, and keep per-piece prices at or below ₹2,500 until then.

Filing means GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns. The QRMP scheme cuts these from 24 filings a year to 8 for turnover up to ₹5 crore ([AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/gst-filing-india-guide)), and CA fees for a small proprietorship are commonly quoted at **₹500–₹1,500 a month** ([TAXAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=766); [RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/cost-of-hiring-ca-in-india)). For income tax, a proprietor can use presumptive taxation, under which **6% of digital receipts is deemed profit** up to ₹2–3 crore of turnover ([Tax2win](https://tax2win.in/guide/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme)); from 1 April 2026 this sits in Section 58 of the new Income-tax Act 2025 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/presumptive-taxation-residents-1961-act-via-a-vis-2025-act.html)). Dropshipping margins can fall below 6%, and declaring less than that means keeping full books and possibly a tax audit, so ask your CA which route fits.

Your website must also meet consumer and labelling rules, and a supplier shipping the goods doesn't move these duties off you. The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 require your legal name, address, customer-care contacts and a named grievance officer, who must **acknowledge complaints within 48 hours and resolve them within one month** ([Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/news/e-commerce-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020-notified)), along with published return, refund, shipping and payment policies, the total price with its breakup, and country of origin ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/976876/e-commerce-rules-2020)); consent boxes must never be pre-ticked ([K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/consumer/dark-patterns-in-the-digital-economy/)). Under the Legal Metrology rules, every product page needs the maker's or packer's name and address, country of origin, generic name, net quantity, MRP and consumer-care details ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/compliances-for-website-e-commerce-website/)); online sellers have been warned of fines up to ₹1 lakh for missing country of origin ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/money/e-tailers-can-be-fined-rs-1-lakh-failing-display-country-origin-128355)), and since 1 July 2026 stores selling imported goods also need a country-of-origin filter ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/21/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2026-explained/)). The dark-pattern guidelines ban false urgency, hidden fees and similar tricks; the regulator has penalised platforms and treated a **recurring 24-hour countdown timer as false urgency** ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/law-firms/view-point/from-nudge-to-notice-what-indias-dark-pattern-orders-mean-for-digital-business)) — exactly the kind of timer many dropshipping apps install. Finally, the DPDP data-protection rules, notified on 13 November 2025, bring their main duties into force around **13 May 2027** ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/)), and the government has proposed pulling that forward to November 2026 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm)), so write a plain privacy notice and a separate, unticked marketing consent now.

## Meta ads cost ₹400–₹2,000 per order, so price decides survival

Meta (Facebook and Instagram) traffic in India is cheap to show but not cheap to convert. E-commerce CPM (the cost to show an ad 1,000 times) typically runs **₹80–₹250** ([productgrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/tools/marketing/meta-ads/)), but CPA runs **₹400–₹2,000 per order** with ROAS of 2.2–3.8x ([Mathew Digital](https://mathewdigital.com/meta-ads-cost-in-india/)), and one 2026 launch playbook tells new brands to expect 1.5–2.5x ROAS and no ad profit in month one ([AimnLaunch](https://aimnlaunch.com/how-to-launch-a-d2c-brand-in-india-in-2026-the-complete-playbook-from-zero-to-rs-10l-month/)). Festive CPMs run **30–50% higher** ([Bizeract](https://bizeract.com/tools/meta-ad-cost-estimator)). Metro audiences cost 20–40% more than Tier-2 cities ([Apex Influence](https://apexinfluence.in/meta-ads)), but Tier-2 and Tier-3 buyers return COD parcels far more often, so cheaper clicks there are not cheaper customers. Bot traffic in India is reported to run 20–30% higher than in richer markets ([AdAmigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-cpm-cpc-benchmarks-by-country-2026)), and 18% GST is added to ad spend, which you reclaim once GST-registered ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/facebook-advertising-pricing/)).

A typical funnel shows why cheap products fail. With a CPM of ₹150, a CTR (the share of viewers who click) of 1.5%, and a CVR (the share of visitors who buy) of 1.5% — close to home-and-living's 1.4% ([Cognito IT](https://cognitoitconsultancy.com/ecommerce-conversion-rate/)) — each order costs **about ₹667 in ads**. A best case (₹100 CPM, 2% CTR, 2% CVR) gives about ₹250, and a weak case (₹200 CPM, 1.2% CTR, 1% CVR) about ₹1,667. For your first tests, spend ₹300–₹500 a day per ad set ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/facebook-advertising-pricing/)), test one or two products at a time ([ProductLair](https://productlair.com/blog/how-much-to-spend-on-dropshipping-ads)), let each ad run 3–4 days before judging it ([AliDrop](https://www.alidrop.co/blogs/facebook-ads-for-dropshipping-strategies-that-work)), and kill it once it has spent 1.5–2× your break-even CPA without a sale ([DropshipXL](https://dropshipxl.com/facebook-ads-for-dropshipping/)). For the ₹1,499 bundle below, that means stopping at about ₹900–₹1,200 spent with no order.

Creative is the real bottleneck, and in India it is cheap to buy. UGC videos (user-generated content: short, phone-shot videos by ordinary creators) cost about **₹1,500–₹8,000 each** ([GetCollab](https://getcollab.in/blog/ugc-creator-rates-india)), with rights to use them in paid ads adding 30–150% ([Influencers-Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/indias-ugc-rates-hit-175-usage-fees-double-the-cost/)). Nano creators (1,000–10,000 followers) charge ₹1,000–₹12,000 per Reel and micro creators ₹8,000–₹80,000 ([Grynow](https://www.grynow.in/blog/influencer-marketing-cost-in-india.html)), but many nano creators accept free product instead of cash ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/nano-influencers-a-not-so-nano-tool-for-influencer-marketing-120778.html)). Buy 6–8 UGC videos, send free product to 10–15 nano home-décor creators, and skip paid influencer Reels until a product is proven.

Leave Google for later. Google Shopping clicks cost about ₹5–₹15 ([Owlclaw](https://owlclaw.com/benchmarks/google-shopping-benchmarks/)), but its automated Performance Max campaigns need about ₹50,000 a month to work well, and below 50 conversions a month plain Standard Shopping often beats them ([Balistro](https://www.balistro.com/blog/performance-marketing-channels-ranked-d2c-india); [eChai](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-google-ads/what-is-performance-max-should-i-run-it)), so add a small Standard Shopping campaign only once a product converts. Research products for free: use the Meta Ad Library set to India and look for ads running 14+ days with 5+ variations, a sign the advertiser is making money ([AdMapix](https://www.admapix.com/blog/app-going-global/find-winning-products-facebook-ads-library)), then check Amazon.in best sellers, Meesho prices and Google Trends. Skip paid spy tools at $39–$199 a month ([Minea](https://www.minea.com/competitor-analysis/competitor-spy-tools/dropshipio-vs-minea)); they are built around TikTok and US data, and TikTok has been blocked in India since 2020 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2012939)).

## A ₹1,499 bundle breaks even at about ₹608 of ads per order

This worked example models a ₹1,499 bundle — five premium cushion covers plus a table runner, say — in a parcel under 1 kg, counted per 100 orders placed, because Meta counts a sale when the order is placed, including COD orders that later come back. It assumes 5% GST, which holds at or below ₹2,500 per piece ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/impact-rate-rationalization-gst-2-0-regime-textile-sector.html)); a ₹450 landed product cost (30% of the price); ₹20 packing; ₹95 freight for two Zone D 500 g slabs at the midpoint of ₹38–₹57 ([shipagent.in](https://www.shipagent.in/rate-calculator); [ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/delhivery-courier-charges)); a ₹40 COD fee ([Delhivery rate card](https://www.scribd.com/document/502572765/Delhivery-Rate-Card)); 4% prepaid payment fees (Razorpay 2% plus Shopify 2%); and an order mix of 60% COD to 40% prepaid, near the ~62.5% national COD share ([ProfitBox360](https://www.profitbox360.com/research/india/ecommerce/cod-prepaid-rto-rates-india)). RTO runs 30% on COD and 3% on prepaid, and each RTO costs freight both ways, the lost packing and a 10% damage allowance on the product. All costs are shown before GST, because a registered store reclaims GST on its inputs.

| Line | How it is calculated | Per 100 orders placed | Per delivered order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orders delivered | 60 COD × 70% + 40 prepaid × 97% | 80.8 delivered, 19.2 returned | — |
| Sales after GST | 80.8 × ₹1,427.62 (₹1,499 ÷ 1.05) | ₹1,15,352 | ₹1,428 |
| Product cost | 80.8 × ₹450 | −₹36,360 | −₹450 |
| Packing | 80.8 × ₹20 | −₹1,616 | −₹20 |
| Forward shipping | 80.8 × ₹95 | −₹7,676 | −₹95 |
| COD fees | 42 delivered COD orders × ₹40 | −₹1,680 | −₹21 |
| Prepaid payment fees | 38.8 × 4% × ₹1,499 | −₹2,326 | −₹29 |
| RTO losses | 19.2 × (₹95 + ₹95 + ₹20 + ₹45 damage) | −₹4,896 | −₹61 |
| **Contribution before ads** | | **₹60,798** | **₹752** |
| Ads at ₹450 per placed order | 100 × ₹450 | −₹45,000 | −₹557 |
| **Profit before fixed costs** | | **₹15,798** | **₹196** |
| Same, if ads cost the typical ₹667 per order | 100 × ₹667 | **−₹5,902** | **−₹73** |

The bundle can spend **₹608 per placed order on ads** (₹60,798 ÷ 100) before it starts losing money, a break-even ROAS of **1,499 ÷ 608 ≈ 2.5x** on the revenue Meta reports. Ignore RTO, as many beginners do, and the same maths suggests a break-even of 1.8x, which means a store celebrating a 2x ROAS in Ads Manager is in fact losing money. At a ₹450 CPA, each delivered order earns about ₹196 before fixed costs; at the typical ₹667 CPA, each loses ₹73. Covering fixed costs at ₹158 profit per placed order takes about **21–27 orders a month on the lean stack** (₹3,300–₹4,300 fixed) or **52–75 orders a month on the recommended stack** (₹8,200–₹11,900 fixed).

| Scenario (same assumptions unless stated) | Break-even ad cost per order | Break-even ROAS |
|---|---|---|
| ₹1,499 bundle, 30% COD RTO (base case) | ₹608 | 2.5x |
| ₹1,499 bundle, COD RTO cut to 22% by confirmation and nudges | ₹660 | 2.3x |
| ₹1,499 bundle, festive COD RTO of 40% | ₹543 | 2.8x |
| ₹1,499 bundle, if Shopify's 2% also applies to COD orders | ₹595 | 2.5x |
| ₹1,499 bundle, RTO ignored (the beginner's mistake) | ₹815 | 1.8x |
| ₹999 product in the same parcel | ₹355 | 2.8x |
| ₹599 product in the same parcel | ₹153 | 3.9x |

Three practical rules follow from these scenarios. Price at ₹1,299 or more, because shipping and RTO are charged per parcel rather than per rupee of sale, so a ₹599 product cannot cover the ₹400+ that Indian ads usually cost per order. Keep landed cost at or below 30–35% of the selling price. And treat every point of COD RTO you remove as money: cutting COD RTO from 30% to 22% adds ₹52 to the ad cost each order can bear. Judge ads on delivered revenue each week, and aim for a CPA of about ₹470 or less, which leaves a 1.3× cushion under break-even for refunds and fixed costs.

## Build through Diwali, test through the wedding season

Timing matters this month. Amazon's Great Indian Festival started on 8 October and Flipkart's Big Billion Days on 9 October ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/amazon-great-indian-festival-2026-to-begin-october-8-prime-members-to-get-up-to-10-extra); [91mobiles](https://www.91mobiles.com/hub/flipkart-big-billion-days-sale-2026-date-offers-revealed/)), Diwali falls around 8 November 2026 ([Wilsonville](https://wilsonvilleoregon.gov/administration/page/diwali)), and ad prices run 30–50% higher through this window while RTO peaked in November last year. Supplier vetting alone takes two to three weeks, so a pre-Diwali paid launch is not realistic. Build and source now, post organically through Diwali, and begin paid tests around mid-November, as wedding-season gifting and home-refresh demand continues into December ([CAIT](https://cait.in/wedding-season-2025-to-generate-₹6-5-lakh-crore-business-from-46-lakh-weddings-across-india-cait-delhi-alone-to-witness-₹1-8-lakh-crore-trade-from-4-8-lakh-weddings-indian/)). Because November RTO runs high, lean hardest on prepaid offers and partial COD in the first test round.

## Ten mistakes that sink Indian dropshipping stores

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Shipping COD orders without confirming them | COD RTO averages 24–26%, and each RTO wastes freight both ways plus the ad spend that won the order ([ProfitBox360](https://www.profitbox360.com/research/india/ecommerce/cod-prepaid-rto-rates-india)) | WhatsApp confirmation within 5 minutes; cancel unconfirmed orders; prepaid discount; partial COD |
| Picking ₹299–₹699 products | They can afford only ₹150–₹355 of ads per order, below typical Indian CPAs | Bundles at ₹1,299 or more |
| Sourcing per order from AliExpress or China | No COD, 7–40-day transit, heavy duty | Domestic makers; bulk import later, if ever |
| Trusting Meta's reported ROAS | Meta counts COD orders that later come back, so you need 2.5x, not 1.8x | Track delivered revenue weekly |
| Spreading a small budget over many products, or judging ads within hours | No reliable data | 1–2 products at a time, 3–4 days each, kill at 1.5–2× break-even CPA |
| Launching paid tests at the Diwali peak | CPMs run 30–50% higher, and RTO peaked at 39.2% in November 2025 ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)) | Build now; test from mid-November with prepaid nudges |
| Paying big annual or lifetime supplier fees upfront | Roposo shut down, and Baapstore's plans keep changing | Low-fee tiers, test orders first, exportable invoices |
| Fake countdown timers, stock counters or pre-ticked add-ons | Illegal dark patterns that the regulator has penalised ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/law-firms/view-point/from-nudge-to-notice-what-indias-dark-pattern-orders-mean-for-digital-business)) | Honest offers, with fees shown upfront |
| Delaying GST registration and gateway KYC | Inter-state sales need GST from the first sale, and late registration is a named beginner mistake ([SeekhoBecho](https://www.seekhobecho.com/blog/why-dropshipping-fails-in-india/)) | Settle the GSTIN route in week one |
| Shipping pillows in boxes | Billed at 14.4 kg instead of 3.6 kg vacuum-packed | Vacuum-pack pillows; flat-fold curtains |

## Conclusion

The research changes what you are actually choosing. In India in 2026, a dropshipping store is less a product hunt than a logistics-and-trust business: parcel size, dispatch speed and COD discipline decide the margin more than any "winning product", which is why your existing curtain and pillow supply matters more than any catalog app. Roposo's closure has removed the easy path for sellers with no supply of their own and tilted the field toward owners who can control quality and ship within 48 hours. Because a well-run ₹1,499 bundle leaves only about ₹196 per delivered order after ads, the edge comes from small operational gains — confirming every COD order, vacuum-packing, prepaid nudges and honest pricing — far more than from clever ads.

Plan for success to look different from where you start. A bundle that works will push you to hold stock of winners, file a trademark and order private-label lots of 50–100 pieces per colourway, so the dropshipping test effectively becomes a direct-to-consumer brand for GD Home Fabric's own category. Treat the day-90 review as a real decision: if no bundle reaches about ₹608 per order after ten honest tests, the problem is the price band or the offer, and more ad spend will not fix it. Re-check every price and legal point flagged above before you commit money.
